Choosing a white noise machine is simpler than the listings make it look. You need a sound that runs continuously without an audible loop, a volume control that goes genuinely quiet, a UK plug or a battery that lasts the night, and the kind of noise you find restful. A timer, a choice of colours and portability matter to some people and not others. Dozens of nature sounds, app control and mood lighting matter to almost nobody.

This page goes through the features one at a time so you can decide which apply to you, then looks at whether a phone app or a fan would do the job for free. The main guide to the [best white noise machines](@sleep-products/best-white-noise-machines-uk) covers how masking works, who a machine suits, and the product examples.

## Feature by feature

### Non-looping sound

The most important feature and the one listings are vaguest about. Cheap digital machines play a short recording on repeat, and once you notice the join you will hear it every time. Look for machines that describe their noise as generated rather than recorded, or choose a fan-based machine, where a real fan makes the sound and looping is impossible. Nature sounds such as rain or waves are recordings on almost every machine and will loop; treat those as a bonus, not the reason to buy.

### Noise colour

White noise is a flat hiss across all frequencies. Pink noise has more low-frequency energy and sounds like steady rain or wind. Brown noise leans lower still, like a distant waterfall. Many people find white noise harsh and settle on pink or brown for sleep. A fan-based machine gives you one soft, fan-like sound; a digital machine gives you a choice. If you have not tried the deeper tones, read [brown noise for sleep](@sleep-sounds/brown-noise-for-sleep) and [white noise for sleep](@sleep-sounds/white-noise-for-sleep) to understand the difference before you commit to a machine with only one.

### Volume range

Check the minimum, not the maximum. A machine that is too loud at its lowest setting is useless for a quiet room and encourages you to run it louder than is sensible. A dial or fine steps is better than three fixed levels. All-night sound at high volume is not good for your hearing, so the ability to go low is a safety feature as much as a comfort.

### Timer versus all-night

A timer suits people who only need help falling asleep or whose partner dislikes the sound. If you are masking noise, you want all-night running, and you should check the machine does not switch itself off after an hour by default. The question of whether all-night sound is wise is covered in [is it bad to sleep with white noise on all night](@sleep-sounds/is-it-bad-to-sleep-with-white-noise-on).

### Power and the UK plug

Many well-known machines were designed for North America. Make sure the listing is the UK version with a three-pin plug, or that a UK adapter is included. Rechargeable machines avoid the plug issue but must reliably last the night; a machine that dies at 3am is worse than none.

### Portability

If you travel, stay in hotels or sleep in shared rooms, a small rechargeable machine you can pack is worth having as well as, or instead of, a mains one. Expect a thinner sound at higher volumes, which matters less at the low levels you should be using anyway.

### Lights and controls

A bright status LED undoes some of the good of a dark room. Choose a machine with no light or one that can be turned off. Buttons or a dial you can operate by touch in the dark are better than a touch panel or an app you need to unlock a phone for.

### Things you can ignore

Bluetooth speakers built into the machine, colour-changing lamps, voice assistant support and long menus of sounds. None of these helps you sleep; some of them keep the phone in the bedroom, which works against you.

## Machine, app or fan?

A phone app is free and good for finding out whether steady sound helps you at all. The costs are a phone by the bed, notifications, a battery to manage and a thin little speaker. If the app works, you have learned something; a machine is then a tidy, phone-free upgrade rather than a necessity.

A bedroom fan produces a very similar broadband sound for nothing extra and cools you in summer. In winter, a fan running for the noise alone is a draught you may not want. It also has only one sound.

A dedicated machine gives you the best speaker for low, steady sound, no phone, no draught and all-year use. Whether that is worth paying for depends on how much sound helps you, which is why trying the app or the fan first is sensible.

## Babies and children

Many parents use a sound machine in a nursery. Keep it well away from the cot, at a low volume, and follow safer-sleep guidance from the NHS and The Lullaby Trust. There is some concern that loud sound close to a baby for hours could affect hearing, so low and distant is the rule. This guide is written for adults; a health visitor is the right person to ask about a baby's sleep.
