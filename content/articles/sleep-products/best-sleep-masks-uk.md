If you are searching for the best sleep mask in the UK, the short answer is this: buy for fit first. A mask that blocks light beautifully in the shop but lifts off your cheek every time you turn over is worse than a cheap one that stays sealed. The three things that matter are the seal around the nose, the strap, and whether the fabric presses on your eyelids.

For most people that points to a contoured mask with an adjustable strap. Side sleepers and anyone who dislikes pressure on the eyes usually get on best with the moulded eye-cup design. People who want something light for travel, or who sleep warm, often prefer a flat silk mask and accept a little more light leakage. Weighted masks are a matter of taste rather than a proven upgrade.

This guide explains how masks help and where they fall short, what to check before buying, and which types suit which sleepers. The product suggestions come after the advice, because the advice is the useful part.

## What a sleep mask can and cannot do

Light is one of the strongest signals your body clock receives. Bright light in the evening pushes your internal night later, and light in the early morning pulls the day forward. Even with your eyes closed, your eyelids let a surprising amount through, which is why a street light or a summer dawn at 4.30am can wake you long before the alarm. Our guide to [why early-morning light wakes you](@waking-at-night/waking-up-too-early) covers the mechanism in more detail.

A mask deals with that light at the source that matters: your eyes. It travels with you, it costs far less than curtains, and it works in a hotel room, on a night shift or next to a partner who reads on a tablet. Some small studies, mostly in hospital wards where light and noise are hard to control, have found that masks and earplugs together improve reported sleep quality. That is encouraging, but it is not the same as evidence that a mask helps a person whose bedroom is already dark.

The honest limits are these. A mask does nothing for noise, temperature or a racing mind. Some people never get used to something on their face, and a poorly fitting mask can be more disturbing than the light it was meant to block. If you are still weighing up whether one is right for you, [does a sleep mask actually help you sleep](@sleep-questions/does-a-sleep-mask-help-you-sleep) is a fair-minded starting point.

> **In short:** A mask is the cheapest, most portable way to get darkness. It fixes light and only light, and only if it fits.

## The main types of sleep mask

### Flat masks

The classic shape: a single layer of fabric, often with a thin elastic strap. They are light, cheap and easy to pack. The weakness is the nose. Because a flat mask stretches across the bridge rather than around it, a gap opens on either side, and light comes straight in. Flat masks also press on the eyelids, which some people find they cannot ignore.

### Contoured (3D) masks

These have moulded eye cups that sit around the eye socket rather than on it. That does two things. It stops the fabric touching your lashes and lids, which matters for comfort and lets the eyes move freely during REM sleep. And the raised edge follows the shape of the nose more closely, closing the light gap that flat masks leave. Many contoured designs use a wider, flatter strap and adjustable cups, so they can be fitted to different face shapes. The trade-off is bulk: they take up more room in a bag.

### Silk masks

Usually flat, made from mulberry silk and marketed for comfort and breathability. Silk feels cool against the skin, which warm sleepers appreciate, and it is gentle on skin and lashes. Light-blocking depends entirely on how well the flat shape fits your face, so expect some leakage at the nose. Most need hand washing.

### Weighted masks

A flat or lightly shaped mask with a small amount of glass-bead fill, marketed for relaxation in the same spirit as a weighted blanket. Some people find the gentle, even pressure across the forehead soothing. There is very little evidence for any sleep benefit specific to the weight, so treat it as a preference. Weighted masks tend to be warmer and heavier to travel with, and they shift more when you turn on your side.

### Masks with ear cups or built-in speakers

A few designs extend the mask into a wraparound band with pockets for flat speakers, or with padded sides that cover the ears. These are really [sleep headphones](@sleep-products/best-sleep-headphones-uk) with a light-blocking bonus, and they suit people who want audio and darkness in one item. As pure masks, they tend to be warmer and less precise around the nose.

## What to look for

### The seal around the nose

This is the single biggest factor. Look for a shaped nose bridge, a contoured edge or soft padding that follows the curve of your face. If you already own a mask and see light when you glance down, the problem is almost always here. A mask that is slightly too loose leaks; a mask that is too tight gives you a headache. Adjustability solves both.

### Strap type and adjustment

A thin round elastic is the cheapest option and the one most likely to slide up or pull hair. A wide, flat strap spreads the pressure and stays put better on a pillow. Adjustable straps, whether with a slider or hook-and-loop, are worth having because heads vary far more than one-size elastic allows. If you have long hair, check where the fastening sits; a clasp at the back of the head is uncomfortable for back sleepers.

### Eye-cup depth for side sleepers

If you sleep on your side, your face presses into the pillow and a flat mask gets pushed sideways, breaking the seal on the upper side. Contoured cups hold their shape better. Look for cups that are deep enough that you can blink freely with the mask on and that do not collapse when you press your cheek down.

### Fabric and warmth

Foam-backed contoured masks are warmer than thin silk or cotton. If you run hot, or sleep in a warm flat in summer, a lighter fabric may be worth the slight loss in light-blocking. If your room is cold, a snug mask is a minor comfort rather than a problem. Anyone with sensitive skin may prefer natural fibres against the face.

### Washing and care

You are pressing this against your skin for eight hours a night. Check the care label before buying. Many contoured masks can be hand washed and air dried, silk usually needs cool hand washing, and some padded designs cannot be washed at all, which is a reason to avoid them. Removable straps make cleaning easier.

### Safety notes

A mask should never press hard on the eyeball. If you have had recent eye surgery, glaucoma or any condition where eye pressure matters, check with your optician or GP first, and lean towards a contoured design. Masks are not suitable for babies or young children.

## Who a sleep mask suits, and who should skip it

A mask is a good fit if:

- You share a room with someone who reads, scrolls or gets up early with the light on.
- Your bedroom has a street light outside, thin curtains or a summer dawn that wakes you.
- You travel, work shifts or nap in the day.
- You want the cheapest, least permanent way to test whether darkness helps you.

You might skip it if:

- Your room is already dark and your problem is noise, heat or a busy mind. Fix that problem first.
- You genuinely cannot tolerate anything on your face. Curtains or a blind fix the room rather than the person.
- You have an eye condition that makes pressure a concern (ask first rather than skipping altogether).

## Cheaper ways to get a darker room

Before or alongside a mask, it is worth doing the free things. Move the alarm clock or charger with the glowing light out of eyeline. Close the door to a landing light. Draw curtains fully and pin the edge to the wall with a clip if a gap lets in a street light. Turn the phone face down. Our step-by-step guide to [making your bedroom darker](@sleep-environment/how-to-make-bedroom-darker) walks through the room from window to door.

If early light is the real problem and you own the property, [blackout curtains or a blind](@sleep-products/best-blackout-curtains-uk) treat the room permanently and help everyone in it. Many people end up with both: curtains for the bedroom, a mask for travel.

If you find you sleep well in the dark but keep leaving a lamp on out of habit, [is it bad to sleep with the light on](@sleep-questions/is-it-bad-to-sleep-with-the-light-on) explains what dim light overnight does and does not do.

## The short version

Choose a contoured mask with an adjustable flat strap unless you have a specific reason not to. Try it for a week, wash it, and check for light at the nose with your eyes open in a lit room. If it seals and stays put on your side, you have found the one. Everything else in the [sleep products guides](@sleep-products) is secondary to that.
