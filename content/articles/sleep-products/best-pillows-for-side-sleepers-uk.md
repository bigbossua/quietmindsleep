The best pillow for a side sleeper in the UK is the one that keeps your neck in a straight line with your spine while you lie on your side. Because your shoulder holds your head well away from the mattress, that takes more height and more firmness than a back sleeper needs. A pillow that collapses to a pancake by 3am, however lovely it felt at 11pm, is the wrong pillow.

That gives you two questions to answer before you look at a single product: how much height do you need, and which fill will hold it all night without overheating you? The rest of this guide answers both, then covers fill types, body pillows, trial periods, and who should look elsewhere. Product suggestions come at the end.

## Why alignment matters for side sleepers

Lie on your side on a bare mattress and your head drops towards the bed, bending your neck sideways. Lie on a pillow that is too tall and your neck bends the other way. Either way, the muscles and joints of the neck spend seven hours slightly out of line. Most people cope, but if you wake with a stiff neck or a headache that eases by lunchtime, the pillow is the first thing to suspect.

The aim is simple: when you lie on your side, your nose should point straight ahead at the wall, not up at the ceiling or down at the mattress, and your ear, shoulder and hip should sit roughly in a line. The pillow's job is to fill the gap between the mattress and your ear. The size of that gap depends on your shoulder width and on how far you sink into the mattress, which is why a pillow that suits your partner may be wrong for you, and why changing the mattress often changes the pillow you need. Our [mattress buying guide](@sleep-products/mattress-buying-guide-uk) explains the sinkage side of that equation.

> **In short:** Side sleepers need a pillow tall and firm enough to hold the head level with the spine all night. Height first, then fill, then everything else.

## Loft and firmness, in plain English

Loft is the pillow's height when your head is on it, not when it is plumped on the bed. Manufacturers describe pillows as low, medium or high loft, but the labels are not standardised, so use them as a rough guide only. Broad-shouldered people on firm mattresses generally need high loft; slighter people on soft mattresses they sink into need less.

Firmness is how much the pillow resists compression. A soft, high pillow is a contradiction for a side sleeper, because your head squashes it flat and the height disappears. Look for medium-firm to firm. A pillow you can fold in half and that stays folded has already given up.

Adjustable pillows solve the guesswork. These have a zipped cover with removable foam pieces, layers or fibre, so you can take out fill until the height is right. They are the most forgiving choice if you are not sure what you need.

## Fill types compared

### Memory foam

A solid block or shredded pieces of viscoelastic foam. It contours to the head and holds its height reliably, which is why it is the most common recommendation for side sleepers. The trade-offs are heat, since dense foam traps warmth, and the faint chemical smell some new foam pillows have for the first few days. Shredded and adjustable versions sleep cooler and can be tuned for height.

### Latex

Natural or synthetic latex foam is springier than memory foam, holds its shape for years and sleeps a little cooler because it is usually pierced with air channels. It is heavier and generally costs more, and some people dislike the bouncy feel. A good long-term option for side sleepers who found memory foam too warm or too sinking.

### Down and feather

Natural fills that feel luxurious and mould around the head. The weakness for side sleepers is that they compress under the head's weight and need re-plumping in the night. A firm, generously filled feather pillow can work, and some people stack two. Not suitable for allergy sufferers without a proper barrier cover.

### Microfibre and hollowfibre

Synthetic fibre pillows are cheap, washable and hypoallergenic, but most flatten quickly and few are firm enough for side sleeping over the long term. A firm-rated microfibre pillow is a reasonable budget choice if you are prepared to replace it every year or so.

### Body pillows

A long pillow you hug, running from head to knees, that supports the top arm and leg as well as the head. Many side sleepers, and most people in later pregnancy, find one takes strain off the shoulder and hips. You still need the head section to be the right height, so treat it as an addition rather than a replacement.

## What to look for

- **Height you can adjust or that matches your build.** If in doubt, buy adjustable.
- **Firmness described as medium-firm or firm.** Soft is for back and front sleepers.
- **A breathable cover you can remove and wash.** Foam cannot go in the machine; the cover can.
- **Trial period and return terms.** Many UK pillow brands offer a trial of several weeks or more. A pillow takes a week or two to judge properly, so a trial is worth more than any description.
- **Heat.** If you already [wake up hot at night](@waking-at-night/waking-up-hot-at-night), avoid solid dense foam or choose a version with a cooling layer, and read the [cooling pillows guide](@sleep-products/best-cooling-pillows-uk) before deciding.
- **Weight and size.** UK standard pillows are around 50 by 75 cm; check your pillowcases fit if you choose a larger or contoured shape.

## Using a trial period well

A pillow trial is only useful if you use it properly. Sleep on the new pillow every night for at least a week before forming a view, because the first two or three nights on any new pillow feel wrong. Keep your old pillow in the cupboard rather than the bin. Pay attention to how your neck feels twenty minutes after getting up, not the moment you wake, since a little morning stiffness fades fast on a good pillow and lingers on a bad one. If you use an adjustable pillow, change the fill in small steps and give each height two or three nights. Note the return deadline the day the pillow arrives.

## Who this suits, and who should look elsewhere

A high, firm pillow suits committed side sleepers, people with broad shoulders, and anyone who wakes with a stiff neck that improves during the day.

It is the wrong choice if you sleep mainly on your back or front, where a high pillow pushes the chin towards the chest. Combination sleepers who switch between side and back often do best with a medium loft and a squashable fill, or with an adjustable pillow set somewhere in between.

If you have persistent neck pain, numbness or tingling in the arms, or pain that wakes you regardless of pillow, that is a conversation for a GP or physiotherapist rather than a shopping decision. The NHS back pain pages are a sensible first read.

## Free things to try first

Before buying, try folding a towel and placing it under your current pillow to add height, or under the pillowcase if the pillow is too soft at the edge. If the towel version fixes your morning neck, you know you need more loft. Try a spare pillow between the knees for the hips. And check the mattress: a sagging mattress tilts the whole spine and no pillow can correct for it.
