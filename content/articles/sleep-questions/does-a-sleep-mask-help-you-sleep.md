Yes, for a lot of people, and for a simple reason: light reaching your eyes at night makes sleep lighter and more broken, and a sleep mask is the cheapest, most portable way to stop it. If your bedroom is genuinely dark already, a mask will do very little. If it is not, and most British bedrooms are not, a mask can make a noticeable difference to how often you wake and how you feel in the morning.

That is the direct answer. The rest of this page covers what the research actually shows, who gains most, the downsides nobody mentions on the packaging, and how to pick a mask you will keep wearing.

## What the evidence says

Two lines of evidence support masks. The first is about light itself. Light is the strongest signal your body clock uses, and light at night, even at fairly low levels, suppresses melatonin and pushes sleep towards its lighter stages. A dark room is one of the least controversial pieces of sleep advice there is, and a mask is a way of carrying a dark room with you.

The second is about masks specifically, and here the studies are small. Research in hospital settings, where lights and noise are constant, has found that patients given eye masks and earplugs report better sleep and show more normal melatonin patterns. A 2023 study by Greco and colleagues, published in the journal Sleep, had healthy adults wear a mask overnight for several nights and found they performed better on memory and alertness tasks the following day than after nights without one. The study was short and involved fewer than a hundred people, so it is encouraging rather than conclusive.

So the honest summary is: strong evidence that darkness helps sleep, modest evidence that a mask delivers enough darkness to matter. Together they add up to "worth trying, especially if you can see your hand in front of your face at 3am".

## Who benefits most

**Anyone with light coming in.** Street lights, a security light next door, thin or ill-fitting curtains, a landing light left on for children. Our guide to [making your bedroom darker](@sleep-environment/how-to-make-bedroom-darker) covers the room-level fixes; a mask is the personal-level one.

**People who wake early in summer.** British dawn in June arrives before five, and light through the eyelids is a very effective alarm. Anyone who finds themselves [waking too early](@waking-at-night/waking-up-too-early) in the lighter months often finds a mask solves it.

**Couples on different schedules.** One partner reading or up at five; the other trying to sleep through it.

**Shift workers and nappers.** Sleeping in daylight is the hardest case, and a mask plus blackout blinds is the standard advice.

**Travellers.** Hotel curtains, planes, a relative's spare room. A mask weighs nothing.

**People who find gentle pressure soothing.** For some, the feeling of a mask is part of a wind-down routine, a small signal that the day is over. This is about habit rather than light, but it works.

## The downsides

Masks are low-risk, but not free of drawbacks.

**Pressure and heat.** A flat mask presses on the eyelids and lashes, which some people cannot bear, and any mask adds warmth to the face on a hot night. Contoured masks with a recess for the eyes solve the first problem; lighter fabrics help with the second.

**Light leaks.** A poor fit lets light in around the nose, which is more irritating than no mask at all.

**Side sleepers.** Anything with bulk gets pushed out of position when you turn over. Slim, contoured designs and soft straps are more forgiving.

**Dry eyes and skin.** Some people find a mask dries the eyes or irritates the skin; a silk or soft cotton surface and regular washing help.

**Missing the morning light.** Light in the morning is what tells your body clock to start the day. If you sleep with a mask, take it off as soon as you wake and get some daylight, or you lose that signal. This matters more for people who struggle with mornings than for those who spring up.

> **In short:** A mask helps if light is reaching you and does little if it is not. Choose one that fits your face and your sleeping position, and take it off the moment you wake.
